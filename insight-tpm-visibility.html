Operational Excellence · Prior Consulting Engagement

39 sites had sponsorship. They didn't have visibility.

A global powertrain manufacturer's Total Productive Maintenance program had exactly what most transformation programs wish for: executive backing, budget, and buy-in across 39 plants. What it didn't have was a way to know which sites were actually improving, and which were just reporting activity.

This engagement was delivered by Patrick Munduga at a global management consulting firm, prior to founding Rockhouse Consulting — not a Rockhouse engagement.

The assumption

Leadership's working assumption was reasonable on its face: give the program executive sponsorship and a shared TPM playbook, and consistent results across the network should follow. Sponsorship was real. Results weren't consistent.

What was actually happening

Without a unified way to see performance across sites, "on track" quietly became a matter of local interpretation. Some plants tracked availability and downtime with real rigor. Others reported softer, generously-rounded numbers that looked fine in isolation but told corporate leadership almost nothing reliable when compared site to site — because they weren't actually measuring the same thing the same way.

39 plants with a shared mandate isn't the same as 39 plants speaking a shared language.

That gap — not effort, not commitment — was what was actually limiting the program. Plants weren't underperforming out of a lack of will. Corporate simply had no reliable way to see which ones needed help and which had already solved the problem worth replicating elsewhere.

What we did

I supported a unified analytics and governance layer for the program — working as a Power BI translator/analyst to standardize how availability, downtime, and improvement actions were defined and measured across the tracked lines feeding the platform, and helping build the reporting cadence that let corporate leadership finally compare progress on the same terms, rather than on each site's own narrative.

What changed

Verified availability hours across the tracked lines climbed from 495 to 5,845 — a 12x increase — as the number of lines in focus grew from 58 to 95 over about two months. (Those hours are scoped to the tracked lines feeding the platform, not the full 39-site network.) But the more durable change was structural: the program shifted from subjective, site-by-site storytelling toward objective, comparable management — leadership could finally see progress on the same terms across the network instead of 39 separate opinions about it.

That's the pattern worth generalizing: sponsorship and budget get a transformation program funded. Visibility and governance are what determine whether it's actually one program at all.
